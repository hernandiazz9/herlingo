and stays untouched; this note only says how the **Russian** pack differs. Every Russian content

## Learner and goal
- Same learner (native Spain Spanish speaker, UI in Spain Spanish with "tú"), **total beginner
  in Russian**, who cannot read Cyrillic yet. Goal: **travel and everyday life** (greetings,
  numbers, introducing yourself, home and routine, transport, hotel, food, shopping, the city,
  health).
- **Level A1 only** in plan 009, with the full French volume. The pack is levelled like English
  (`levels: ["A1"]`) so A2 can be added later with the same molds.
- Oral first, like every pack: audio on every item, short phrases, grammar only where it helps to
  speak. Russian adds a reading layer (Cyrillic + romanization) on top.

## Volume and format
Same format as French (docs/content-guide.md): **~420 phrases, ~320 vocabulary words, ~80 Q&A,
an alphabet unit + 10 units + a grammar unit, ~50 lessons (8–30 items each), ~10 grammar cards,
12 dialogues, 12 stories, 8 placement questions**. All `level: "A1"`.

## Cyrillic, stress marks and romanization (generic code, plan 009 T111–T113)
- **Everything in Cyrillic with the stress marked**: every target-language word of two or more
  syllables carries U+0301 COMBINING ACUTE ACCENT after its stressed vowel (молоко́, спаси́бо,
  до свида́ния); `ё` is always written as `ё` and is stressed by itself, never marked again.
  One-syllable words carry no mark. `content:check` enforces it through the pack's `stress` rule.
- **Romanization under the Cyrillic**, generated by code from the pack's `romanization` table
  (no per-item field): a **Spanish-reader scheme** (х → j, ж → zh, ш → sh, щ → shch, ч → ch,
  ц → ts, ы → y, й → i, ь → ', е → e / ye at word start and after a vowel, ё → io / yo,
  ю → iu / yu, я → ia / ya), the stressed vowel gets a Spanish acute. It transliterates
  letters and does not respell vowel reduction (`спаси́бо` → `spasíbo`, `молоко́` → `molokó`);
  how it really sounds goes in the pronunciation tips. The learner hides it with a switch on Perfil ("Mostrar transliteración"), stored
  per language on the device; on by default.
- **Typed answers accept Cyrillic or Latin.** A typed Latin answer is compared with the
  romanization of the expected answer, tolerant to the usual alternatives (kh/j, ya/ia, yu/iu,
  yo/io, zh, y/i), with the same typo tolerance as today. Stress marks never count (missing or
  wrong marks are not an "acento" error), and `ё` = `е`.
- Speech recognition (`ru-RU`) returns Cyrillic without marks: scoring ignores marks and `ё`/`е`.
- TTS gets the text without stress marks (the marks are for the eyes).

## Language rules
- **Standard Russian (ru-RU)**, natural and current, short phrases (≤ 10 words when possible).
  Prices in rubles (₽), 24-hour times, dates "7 октября́".
- **Register** (`formalRegister: "vy"`): `vy` = вы to one person (strangers, staff, older
  people), `tu` = ты (friends, family, children), `neutral` = either. Q&A replies and dialogue
  options match it. The dialogue badge says "Trato de usted" / "Trato de tú", like Italian lei.
- Cases appear from the start inside fixed phrases (Я из Мадри́да, в гости́нице); grammar cards
  explain them later; never a declension table in a lesson.
- Vocab: nouns carry `gender` (`m`, `f`, `n`; plural-only nouns `pl`), no `article` (Russian has
  none). `es` with its Spanish article.
- **Pronunciation tips** (phrase `explanation`) for a Spanish speaker: stress is free and must be
  learned (the marks), unstressed о sounds like а (молоко́ → "malakó") and unstressed е/я like
  и, ы, soft consonants before е/ё/и/ю/я/ь, ж/ш/щ/ч/ц, х = Spanish jota, rolled р is easy,
  final consonants devoice (хлеб → "jlep"), в before voiceless consonants, the "-ого" ending
  read as "-ova".
- **Numbers**: `numberWords` 0–100, hundreds, тысяча; the numbers unit teaches prices
  ("сто два́дцать рубле́й") and times.

## Dialogues and stories
- **Dialogues (12)**, 6–10 turns, ≥ 3 learner turns, 3 options each, Spanish feedback, **one
  native speaker per dialogue** (Russian has two neural voices; the learner's options get the
  other one): passport control, taxi from the airport, hotel check-in, buying a metro card,
  café order, restaurant and the bill, market (asking prices), clothes shop, asking the way,
  museum ticket office, pharmacy, meeting a neighbour (ты vs вы).
- **Stories (12)**, present tense (past tense in the last three), 80–130 words, recurring cast
  in **Saint Petersburg**: **Lucía**, a Spanish woman from Seville learning Russian like you,
  visiting her friend **А́ня** (27, museum guide), Anya's brother **Ди́ма** (22, student, drives a
  taxi at weekends) and their neighbour **Ве́ра Петро́вна** (retired teacher). Arrival,
  the flat, breakfast, the metro, the market, a museum, a café, white nights by the river, a cold
  and the pharmacy, a dacha weekend, the past week (past tense), goodbye at the airport.
